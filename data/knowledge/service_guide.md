# JanaVaani Public Service Guide

This guide explains which department handles which problem, what to include in a complaint, and how long it usually takes. Departments and agencies are illustrative for the demo; a real deployment must follow the official GHMC / HMWSSB / TGSPDCL jurisdiction mapping. Section anchors are used as citations by the guide assistant.

## Roads and Municipal Engineering
Handles potholes, broken or caved-in roads, damaged footpaths, road cuts left open after pipeline or cable work, and broken speed breakers. Illustrative agency: GHMC Engineering.
**Include:** the exact spot or a landmark (bus stop, temple, school, metro pillar number), how big the damage is (small, large, very large), since when it has been there, and whether it is blocking traffic. A photo helps the engineer estimate the material needed.
**Typical SLA:** 96 hours for medium priority; 48 hours when the damage causes accidents or blocks traffic.
**Examples:** "Big pothole near Miyapur X Roads, bikes are skidding"; "Road dug for cables in Uppal two months ago and never relaid".

## Sanitation and Solid Waste
Handles garbage not collected, overflowing bins, streets not swept, debris dumping and dead animals on the road. Illustrative agency: GHMC Solid Waste Management.
**Include:** the location, the type of waste, since when it has not been collected, and whether a public bin is overflowing.
**Typical SLA:** 96 hours; dead animals and waste causing disease risk are treated as high priority (48 hours).
**Examples:** "Garbage not lifted in our lane since Monday"; "Dead dog lying near the ration shop".

## Water Supply
Handles no water supply, low pressure, pipeline leaks and contaminated or discoloured drinking water. Illustrative agency: HMWSSB.
**Include:** the type of problem (no supply, leak, contamination, low pressure), since when, and how many households are affected.
**Typical SLA:** 48 hours for no supply; contaminated water that makes people sick is treated as critical (24 hours) because it is a health risk.
**Examples:** "No water for three days in Tarnaka"; "Muddy water in the morning supply, children have stomach problems".

## Streetlights
Handles streetlights not working, flickering lamps, lights burning during the day, and bent or damaged poles. Illustrative agency: GHMC Electrical.
**Include:** the location, the pole number if visible, how many lights are off, and since when. Dark streets that make people feel unsafe at night are prioritised higher.
**Typical SLA:** 96 hours; a bent pole that may fall is high priority.

## Power Distribution
Handles power cuts, voltage fluctuation, smoking or noisy transformers and low-hanging or sparking lines. Illustrative agency: TGSPDCL. For any live wire or sparking line, call **1912** or **112** first.
**Include:** whether the outage affects only your house, the street or the whole area, since when, and whether there is sparking or danger.
**Typical SLA:** 48 hours for outages; anything with live wires is an emergency.

## Sewerage and Drainage
Handles overflowing drains and sewers, blocked nalas, stagnant drain water and missing manhole covers. Illustrative agency: HMWSSB / GHMC Storm Water Drains.
**Include:** the location, whether sewage is entering homes, and since when. An open manhole is dangerous: mention it clearly.
**Typical SLA:** 48 hours for overflow; sewage entering homes and open manholes are treated as critical.

## Traffic and Road Safety
Handles traffic signals not working, dangerous junctions without zebra crossings, wrong-side driving hotspots and missing or fallen sign boards. Illustrative agency: Traffic Police.
**Include:** the junction or road name, what the problem is, and the time of day it happens.
**Typical SLA:** 48 hours for a failed signal; signage issues 168 hours.

## Public Health
Handles mosquito breeding and missing fogging, dengue or fever clusters, conditions in government hospitals and food safety at stalls. Illustrative agency: GHMC Health / Entomology.
**Include:** the hospital, facility or area, and what the issue is. If many people in one colony have fever, say how many.
**Typical SLA:** 96 hours; disease outbreaks are high priority.

## Government Schools
Handles broken school toilets, leaking or cracked school buildings and missing drinking water for students. Illustrative agency: School Education Department.
**Include:** the school name and the problem. Anything that puts children at risk (cracked roof, falling plaster) is prioritised.
**Typical SLA:** 96 hours; building safety risks 48 hours.

## Town Planning
Handles encroachment of footpaths, parks and government land, and illegal or unauthorised construction. Illustrative agency: GHMC Town Planning.
**Include:** the address of the site and what is happening (encroachment, extra floors, construction on a lake bed).
**Typical SLA:** 168 hours, because inspection and notices take time.

## Other and Unclassified Requests
Pensions, ration cards, certificates and other requests outside these departments are marked "Other" and always go to a human reviewer, who forwards them to the correct office or explains where to apply.

## Emergencies
If there is fire, a live wire, a gas leak, a collapsing wall or building, flooding inside homes or a person in danger, **call 112 immediately** (108 for ambulance, 101 for fire, 1912 for electricity). JanaVaani shows these numbers as soon as it detects emergency words in any language. You can still register the complaint afterwards; it is marked critical and a supervisor must confirm the emergency, after which the SLA is 4 hours.

## How Priority and SLA Work
Every complaint gets a priority score from 0 to 100 that is shown with its reasons: the reported urgency, hazards (each +10, up to +20), citizen distress, vulnerable people affected (+8) and the number of citizens who reported the same issue. Safety rules can only raise the score; an emergency keyword sets a floor of 80. Critical complaints have a 24-hour SLA, high 48 hours, medium 96 hours and low 168 hours. If the SLA is breached the complaint is escalated to the supervisor, and later to the administrator.

## Duplicate Complaints and Community Support
If someone nearby already reported the same problem, JanaVaani shows it and lets you add your support instead of filing a new complaint. More supporters raise the priority, and everyone who supported is notified when it is fixed. You can always file a new complaint if yours is different.

## Appeals and Reopening
When an officer marks a complaint resolved, you are asked to confirm. If it is not fixed, reject the closure: the complaint is reopened and escalated. After a closure you can also file an appeal, which a supervisor decides; you can track the appeal status separately.

## How Verification Works
Officers must upload a proof photo when resolving. JanaVaani checks that the photo was taken near the complaint location and that it is not simply a copy of your original photo. An AI model may compare the before and after photos, but a photo can never prove a repair with certainty, so the final confirmation always comes from you. Suspicious proofs are flagged to the supervisor.

## Privacy and Anonymous Reporting
Phone numbers, Aadhaar, PAN and email addresses are masked before any AI processing, and officers see the masked text. You can report anonymously (officers will not see your name) or privately (your complaint is excluded from public maps and statistics). Public maps are blurred by about 75 metres and never show text or photos of people. All data in this demo is synthetic.
